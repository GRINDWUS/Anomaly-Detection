## 🛡️ 1. NTRO — SecureMailScope (PS #SIH26159)
### *How to explain it to your friend over chai:*

> "Bro, imagine a high-level government officer getting an email that looks 100% real. It says it's from **'PMO India'** or **'Director NTRO'**, there are no spelling mistakes, and the logo is real.
>
> Now, normal spam filters try to read the text inside the email to see if it's fake. But for government intelligence agencies, **you CANNOT let an AI read secret government emails** — that's a massive privacy leak! Plus, smart hackers don't use bad spelling anymore. They trick you by changing tiny hidden things in the domain — like replacing the letter **'o'** with a Russian/Cyrillic **'о'** that looks identical to human eyes.
>
> **What our system does:**
> We built a digital detective called **SecureMailScope**. It never reads what's written inside the email. Instead, it checks the **invisible digital stamps and seals** on the envelope — stuff like security signatures (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), secret server hops, and fake lookalike domain tricks.
>
> Within 0.1 seconds, it tells the officer: *'Bro, don't open this! The letter looks real, but the digital stamp is forged and the domain uses a fake Russian letter.'*
>
> **Why it's a win:** Almost no other team picks this because it sounds super technical with cryptography, but we built a super simple system that wins because it protects government secrets without violating privacy!"

## 🛰️ 2. ISRO — AstraGuard Burn-In Detection (PS #SIH26170)
### *How to explain it to your friend over chai:*

> "Bro, when ISRO builds a satellite worth ₹500 Crores, they put electronic chips inside it. Before launching the satellite into space, they test these chips in a hot oven at 125°C for 7 full days (168 hours) to make sure they won't die in space. This is called a **Burn-In test**.
>
> The problem is, some chips pass the test on Day 1 (0h) and Day 2 (24h), but slowly start leaking electricity and die on Day 7 (168h). If a defective chip gets approved and sent into space, the whole satellite dies in orbit and ₹500 Crores is wasted! But ISRO doesn't want to wait 7 full days for every single chip batch.
>
> **What our system does:**
> We built **AstraGuard**. It looks at the electrical data from just the first **24 hours** of testing and uses physics equations (like how heat degrades metal) to predict: *'Hey ISRO, this chip looks fine right now, but by hour 168 it's going to fail. Throw it out now!'*
>
> **Why it's a win:** We save ISRO 5 full days of testing time per batch and guarantee that zero bad chips ever get launched into space!"

>
> **Why it's a win:** It's super visual, looks like a futuristic video game map, and keeps Indian scientists safe in Antarctica!"

> 2. If a suspicious car drives by, it reads the dirty license plate instantly.
> 3. If a known criminal/terrorist walks past, it scans their face in 0.05 seconds and matches it against a watchlist.
> 4. If someone leaves an unattended bag near a post, it raises an alarm.
>
> **Why it's a win:** It takes away human fatigue and gives soldiers instant alerts so no border breach goes unnoticed!"
